october 6, 2026 ? multiplayer agents

multiplayer agents share one board.

I say multiplayer agents when more than one agent is in the same effort at the same time. The name in a textbook is a multi-agent system. I keep the shorter phrase because the failure is easier to see if you have played a game with other people. You do not each get a private copy of the match.

A single agent can still do many steps. It researches, then it writes, then it moves on. That is one player taking turns. Multiplayer starts when two of them are acting before either has finished, and both believe they know the score. If they are reading different scores, they are not cooperating. They are playing two matches.

The board, in the systems I work on, is a shared record. It holds the demand that was confirmed, the scope that was frozen, and who has already been contacted. An agent can see its own lane and that record. It cannot see inside another agent’s reasoning, and it should not have to. What it cannot do is invent a second board.

This is also why I do not treat “add another agent” as a feature by itself. In a multiplayer match, another player helps only if the rules say what they own and what they are not allowed to redo. Without that, the new player takes a shot someone else already took. The last note in this set is about that miss. The next one is the moment the board goes stale, which is the handoff.
