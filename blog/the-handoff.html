october 20, 2026 ? multiplayer agents

the hard part is the handoff.

When I tell someone outside engineering what I do at VibeGrow, the word agents ends the conversation. The work is easier to picture than the vocabulary. Several pieces of software are supposed to research a market, shape an offer, find customers, and keep the operation running, toward one objective. Each one can do its own step. The failures I keep running into are not failures of intelligence. They are failures of handoff.

In the last post I called this multiplayer, because the agents share a board. Here is where the board goes stale. Research confirms demand in one narrow segment and writes it down. Product starts building against that note. Then the finding gets revised, because the segment was smaller than it looked or the offer has to change. If product is still building against the old note, everyone downstream inherits a decision that is no longer true.

I had already seen that shape away from agents. On the F1TENTH team the car carried LIDAR, an IMU, and a camera, and each one reported its own version of the track. The stack had to agree on one picture fast enough to steer. The sensors were not useless on their own. On the bad runs they had stopped agreeing, and the car acted on a picture the rest of the system had left behind. Most of what I learned came from those runs.

The routing tool made the same demand in a quieter way. Dijkstra’s, A*, and a genetic algorithm can each return a route under traffic and delivery windows. The person dispatching the truck needs one answer they can check. That is why the page was Flask and Folium, not a file only I could read. A result the next person cannot inspect will be done over, and the second answer will not match the first.

Anyone who wants software to run a real process is asking separate systems to share one version of what is true. The coordination layer is that shared record. When it holds, the next step starts from the current decision. When it slips, two parts of the system solve the same problem twice, and a third acts on a premise the others abandoned. The next post is that duplicate, up close.
